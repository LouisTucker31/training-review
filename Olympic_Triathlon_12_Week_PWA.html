A combined view of training demand, recovery, health, energy and performance across 06 Jul 26 - 27 Sep 26, with race day on 26 Sep 26. The aim is to see whether rising training stress was matched by a predictable dip in recovery - and whether race-week tapering produced a rebound.

Overview

Your expectation is partly supported. Training intensity rose clearly, but total activity time did not climb in a straight line. Recovery markers softened most visibly in the final hard weeks, then some markers, especially Training Readiness and resting HR, improved in race week. Sleep score and stress did not show the same clean taper rebound.

Conclusion

Your hypothesis was mostly right. The data is more nuanced than a simple rise-and-fall curve.

Training: intensity rose clearly across the block, but total activity time was variable. The key progression was more race-specific demand, not simply more hours every week. Acute load peaked in W7, weighted intensity peaked in W10, bike volume peaked in W11, and W12's very high activity training load is dominated by the race itself.

Recovery: there is a genuine late-build dip. Training Readiness is ~52 in W10-W11, HRV falls to 75.5 ms in W11, and sleep remains around 6.9 h. In W12, activity time drops from 9h07 in W10 to 5h19 and acute load from 302.4 to 232.1; readiness rebounds to 64.0, HRV improves to 78.3 and resting HR reaches 49.6. Those are the strongest signs that the taper was beginning to work.

Health held up reasonably well: there is no across-the-board deterioration. Resting HR improves over the 12 weeks, HRV remains strong after the opening week, and Garmin performance markers are stable to modestly better. The clearest strain signal is readiness rather than a collapse in baseline cardiovascular metrics.

The caveat: race week is not a clean “fully recovered” week. Stress is highest at 33.0 and sleep score is lowest at 68.7, so some recovery measures worsened even as readiness/RHR improved. Pre-race logistics, competition stress and the race itself can all sit inside that same weekly average.

Overall: the block looks more like fitness preserved/improved while recoverability became temporarily constrained, followed by a partial race-week rebound, rather than health steadily deteriorating as training rose.

Method and data notes

Show methodology and interpretation notes

Weeks run Monday-Sunday. Calories In and Body Weight come from Bevel weekly averages; all other metrics come from Garmin. Calories Out, Active Calories, Steps, Resting HR, Stress, Body Battery, sleep and HRV are Garmin daily values averaged within each week.

Training Readiness is the weekly average after-wake-up score. Acute Load is the corresponding weekly average morning acute-load value. Intensity Minutes use Garmin's moderate + 2 × vigorous convention. Swim, bike and run totals use individual sport legs and exclude multisport parent records to prevent double counting.

Race predictions are Garmin estimates, not results. Pace and speed are aggregate weekly moving-time averages and should not be read as like-for-like performance tests. Bevel W2 contains no body-weight reading and is not interpolated.

The “Training vs recovery” graph normalises each series only for visual shape comparison. It does not create a new health or training score. Correlations shown in this report use 12 weekly observations and are descriptive only.
